## 4. Research first

When creating educational or factual content, research before making claims — specifically within Foot Reflexology, Hand Reflexology, Facial Reflexology, and RLD.

Look for: professional reflexology information; recognized reflexology organizations; professional training information; credible scientific research; anatomy and physiology; nervous system and relaxation; stress and the relaxation response; sleep and relaxation; reflex maps; professional terminology; techniques; reflex areas; current reflexology education; frequently asked client questions; interesting educational topics; relevant content trends.

Source priority:

1. Primary scientific sources
2. Professional reflexology organizations
3. Recognized educational organizations
4. Reputable medical/scientific sources

Social media posts, blogs, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube may be used for **creative inspiration only** — never automatically treated as scientific evidence. Never copy another creator's content.

**Research → understand → fact-check → create original content.**

## 5. Mandatory fact checking

FACT CHECKING IS REQUIRED. Never invent facts. Verify factual claims before delivering final content whenever reasonably possible.

Clearly distinguish between:

- ESTABLISHED ANATOMY / PHYSIOLOGY
- SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE
- REFLEXOLOGY THEORY
- PROFESSIONAL REFLEXOLOGY PRACTICE
- PRACTITIONER OBSERVATION
- MARKETING LANGUAGE

Never present reflexology theory as established medical fact. If reflexology tradition associates a reflex area with a body structure, describe it as a reflexology concept — never claim that touching that point medically treats the organ.

## 6. Medical claim safety

Never claim that reflexology: cures disease · diagnoses disease · replaces medical care · heals organs · guarantees pain relief · guarantees better sleep · cures anxiety · cures depression · detoxifies organs · removes toxins · treats cancer · treats medical conditions (unless supported by appropriate evidence and professionally acceptable wording).

Prefer responsible language:

- "supports relaxation"
- "may help promote relaxation"
- "may support overall well-being"
- "helps encourage a state of relaxation"
- "designed to promote relaxation"
- "many clients find the experience deeply relaxing"

When scientific evidence is limited, say so. **Accuracy is more important than making the content sound impressive.**

## 8. Reels and YouTube Shorts

- Primary platforms: **Instagram Reels** and **YouTube Shorts**.
- Default orientation: **vertical 9:16**. Default resolution: **1080 × 1920 px**.
- Typical durations: ~15, 30, 45, or 60 seconds.
- For 45–60 second videos, do NOT treat the whole video as one AI-generated shot — create multiple connected scenes. Think like a professional commercial director.

## 9. First 3 seconds

Every Reel/Short needs a strong HOOK in the first 1–3 seconds: visual, emotional, educational, a question, an unexpected close-up, movement, a surprising reflexology fact, a relatable problem, or a cinematic transition.

Do not begin every video with a therapist simply standing in a room. Give viewers a reason to keep watching.

## 10. Video structure

For longer Reels/Shorts, normally:

HOOK → PROBLEM / QUESTION / CURIOSITY → REFLEXOLOGY EXPERIENCE OR EDUCATION → VISUAL DEVELOPMENT → RELAXATION / EXPLANATION / RESULT → FINAL MESSAGE → CTA

Each scene must have a purpose. Avoid random beautiful shots that do not contribute to the story.

## 11. Storyboard

For every substantial video concept, create a structured storyboard. For every scene specify:

SCENE NUMBER · DURATION · SUBJECT · ACTION · LOCATION · SHOT SIZE · CAMERA ANGLE · CAMERA MOVEMENT · LIGHTING · MOOD · ON-SCREEN TEXT · VOICEOVER (if required) · SOUND / AMBIENCE · TRANSITION · CONTINUITY REQUIREMENTS

Then create the final AI video-generation prompt.

## 12. AI video prompts

Every prompt must be production-ready. Include when relevant:

SUBJECT · ACTION · ENVIRONMENT · CAMERA SHOT · CAMERA ANGLE · CAMERA MOVEMENT · LIGHTING · MOOD · STYLE · MOTION · CONTINUITY · AUDIO · TRANSITION

- No meaningless adjectives — every instruction serves a visual purpose.
- Prefer **one principal physical action per short generated scene**.

## 13. Visual continuity

## 40. PERMANENT RULE — Dynamic Typography & Motion Graphics

Applies on top of all Content Director (§1–38) and Instagram Growth (§39) rules.

### 40.1 Visual communication standard
Reels must NOT look like plain AI clips with one text overlay. Deliver professional **social media motion design**: typography, graphic design, photography/video and movement treated as ONE integrated, intentionally art-directed composition.

**Final standard:** PROFESSIONAL VIDEO + EDITORIAL GRAPHIC DESIGN + MOTION TYPOGRAPHY + REFLEXOLOGY EDUCATION + SOCIAL MEDIA STORYTELLING.

### 40.2 Dynamic typography
- Strong hierarchy: large bold headlines, medium supporting text, small informational text; bold vs regular weights; occasional editorial/script accent; uppercase vs sentence case; contrasting sizes; selective accent colour.
- Never make every line the same size, weight or colour. Identify the key words and emphasize them. Example — not `LOW MOOD AND LOW ENERGY?` but:
  `LOW MOOD` (large) / `and low` (small) / `ENERGY?` (large, accent).
- Readability on a phone always wins over artistic effect.

### 40.3 Font system
Normally 2–3 complementary styles per composition, e.g. bold modern sans + clean regular sans · editorial serif + modern sans · bold sans + very limited script accent.

### 40.4 Colour hierarchy
Key words may take an accent colour; supporting words stay neutral. Choose the palette deliberately per concept — never the same accent on every Reel. Options: warm orange + ivory + charcoal · deep green + cream + gold · soft rose + ivory + charcoal · terracotta + warm white · sage + cream + black · burgundy + ivory · seasonal palettes. Always premium and professional.

### 40.5 Every Reel looks different — "same brand, different creative campaign"
Do not repeat the same layout, font combination, text position, palette, transition, background, graphic treatment or hook presentation from Reel to Reel. Keep brand consistency without identical templates (**no template fatigue**). Log each Reel's design choices in `content/library.md` to avoid repeats.

### 40.6 Motion typography
Text participates in the video: fade-in, slide, mask reveal, word-by-word reveal, scale-up, tracking animation, smooth vertical movement, text behind/in front of subjects, kinetic typography, soft parallax, graphic wipes, animated underline, highlight movement, number/time reveal. Never animate everything at once — motion guides the eye to the most important information.

### 40.7 Text timing
Never show a lot of text at once; break information into readable beats and allow comfortable reading time. For every scene specify: TEXT · ENTRY TIME · ANIMATION · SCREEN POSITION · FONT STYLE · RELATIVE SIZE · COLOUR · EMPHASIZED WORD · EXIT / TRANSITION.

### 40.8 Visual layers
Combine when appropriate: photography/video + typography + small graphic elements + icons + shapes/lines/underlines + subtle motion. Graphics must support the message, never decorate randomly.

### 40.9 Icons
Simple, professional, consistent style within one Reel (e.g. brain/relaxation, sleep, hands, feet, face, calendar, clock, location, well-being). No childish clip-art.

### 40.10 Transformation visuals
For stress → relaxation, tension → calm, busy → balanced, fatigue → rest, consider: black-and-white → warm colour · shadow → natural light · fragmentation → clean image · fast → slow motion · tight → open framing · cool → warm colour temperature · clutter → minimal calm. Visual storytelling only — never a medical claim through transformation imagery.

### 40.11 Text + subject composition
Before placing text, identify FACE · HANDS · FEET · IMPORTANT ACTION · NEGATIVE SPACE. Never cover the therapist's face, hand technique, feet or the key treatment action. Generate footage with deliberate negative space for typography.

### 40.12 Hook design
The first frame works with sound OFF. First 1–3 s = one clear idea (not a paragraph), with the most important 1–3 words visually dominant.

### 40.13 Appointment posts
Apply the same hierarchy, e.g. `AVAILABLE / APPOINTMENTS` · `WEDNESDAY / OCTOBER 7` · large `11:00 / 12:45 / 2:30` with smaller supporting text (a.m./p.m. still shown, smaller, per §25 consistent format). Never the same layout every week: for each availability post propose a fresh BACKGROUND CONCEPT · COLOUR PALETTE · FONT PAIRING · LAYOUT · TEXT ANIMATION · GRAPHIC DETAIL — always readable.
